How to count walkaways on a busy night

Your POS shows every drink you sold. It doesn’t show the guests who gave up before they ordered. One person with a clipboard can count them in an hour.

DrinkDock teamSeptember 8, 20266 min read

In a 2024 survey of 1,000 U.S. consumers, 61% said they had left a line before reaching their turn.1 At a bar, those guests don’t appear in any report. Counting them takes one person, one peak hour and the sheet at the bottom of this page.

Pick the hour

Open your POS hourly sales report for your busiest night of the week and find the hour with the most drink transactions. That’s the hour to count.

Choose who counts

Someone who isn’t working the bar: a manager, a host or a friend you trust. They need a clear view of the bar and nothing else to do for the hour. Bartenders are too busy to count accurately.

What to count

  1. Joined. Each guest who steps up to the bar to order. When one person orders for a group, count one.
  2. Walked away. Each guest who joined and left the bar without ordering. Count them once they’re clearly gone, not when they step back for a moment.
  3. Wait times. Pick five guests over the hour and time each one from joining to drink in hand.

After the hour, pull three numbers from your POS for the same hour: orders, drinks sold, and the share of drinks that were cans or bottles.

Do the math

Walkaway rate = walked away ÷ joined

Lost drinks = walked away × drinks per order

Lost sales = lost drinks × average drink price

Drinks per order is drinks sold divided by orders, from your POS for that hour. Here’s a worked example with made-up numbers:

InputExample
Guests who joined240
Guests who walked away22
Drinks per order1.6
Average drink price$9

Walkaway rate: 22 ÷ 240 = 9.2%

Lost drinks: 22 × 1.6 ≈ 35

Lost sales: 35 × $9 ≈ $315 in one hour

If 40% of the drinks sold in that hour were cans or bottles, about 14 of those 35 lost drinks were orders that needed no mixing at all. Those are the orders a self-serve machine can take off the bar.

What the count misses

  • Guests who saw the crowd and never joined it. Treat your number as a floor.
  • Guests who waited and then ordered less than they planned: one drink instead of a round, or no second drink.
  • Variation between nights. Count the same hour on two or three nights before you draw conclusions.

What to do with the number
