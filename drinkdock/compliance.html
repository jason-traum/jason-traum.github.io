Built to run inside your license

A vending machine doesn’t change who is responsible for a sale. You are, the same as at the bar. So DrinkDock is designed to keep your staff in control of every sale it makes.

Every sale is your sale

The machine sells your inventory, bought from your distributor, at your prices, under your license. DrinkDock provides the equipment and software. We never hold, buy or resell alcohol.

ID on every alcohol sale

Pennsylvania lists the IDs a licensee may accept in 47 P.S. §4-495. The scanner reads and validates the barcode on U.S. driver’s licenses and state ID cards before payment opens. Expired, damaged or invalid IDs are declined. Passports and military IDs, which don’t carry that barcode, are sent to your staff.

The law gives licensees a good-faith defense when an ID is checked with a transaction scan device (§4-495(g)). The machine keeps a timestamped record of each check for your files.

Minors and visibly intoxicated guests

Under §4-493(1), a licensee may not sell or permit sales to minors or to visibly intoxicated people. An ID scan can’t judge intoxication, so the machine is designed around your staff’s judgment:

  • It installs within sight of the bar, where your bartenders can see who is using it.
  • Each scan allows at most two drinks. The same ID then waits 20 minutes unless a staff member resets it.
  • Staff can lock out a guest’s ID for the rest of the night with one tap.
  • Staff can pause all sales instantly from Manager or the key switch.

Philadelphia already has a self-pour beer bar that works this way: staff check ID up front, and a per-guest limit stops pours until staff reset it (Inquirer, 2023).

Hours

Sales lock to your licensed hours and your Sunday permit, or any earlier cutoff you set. The screen tells guests when sales end for the night.

Your staff and RAMP

Your RAMP-certified staff remain responsible for service on the floor. Every install includes a 20-minute walkthrough on pausing, resetting a limit and locking out an ID.

Scan data

We don’t sell or share ID scan data, which §4-495(h) prohibits anyway. The machine stores only what your records need: the result of the age check, the time and the ID’s expiration date. No ID photos and no marketing profiles.

The PLCB review

Pennsylvania’s Liquor Code doesn’t address alcohol vending machines by name. Before any install, we submit a request for a written advisory opinion from the PLCB describing the exact setup at your venue, and we share the response with you. If the answer is no, we don’t install.

Isn’t this the 2010 wine kiosk?

No. Those were state-run machines in grocery stores. Each sale needed a breathalyzer and a remote state employee watching on video, and the program ended in 2011 after malfunctions and a contract dispute (WHYY).

DrinkDock sits inside a licensed bar or hotel, next to staff who can see every sale.
