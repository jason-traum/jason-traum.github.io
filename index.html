Dreamy

An AI running coach that plans around the days you can't run

Status
Beta ran Feb to Apr 2026
Built
Jan to Apr 2026, while working full time
Beta
15 runners through full race cycles. 14 of the 15 rated it above the coach or app they used before.
Link
getdreamy.run (invite-only)
Built with
Claude Code, TypeScript, Next.js, Postgres, Anthropic API

Dreamy started when I was training for my second marathon. Work got busier, my back was acting up, and every plan I tried fell apart once life got in the way. So I turned to ChatGPT. The models kept giving me bad plans, so I read up on sports science and the top running coaches and kept rewriting my prompt until the answers got good. I ran a PR and shared it with my run club. As the models got better I needed less of the prompt, but I wanted more than a prompt could do. Nothing checked it against the research, you had to be really good at prompting to get something usable, and it didn’t sync with anything or look at your actual runs.

With Dreamy, the plan comes from rules taken from the research, it syncs with Strava and analyzes every run, and the same data always gives you the same plan. The biggest difference is that it works around your schedule. You tell it which days you can't run and it rebuilds the week around them, so you might run seven days one week and four the next. It adapts when you miss a workout or a race moves, and it explains every change and the science behind it, so over time you learn to coach yourself.

I did an absurd amount of research for it. I used AI to find every marathon plan and sports science paper I could, and gave it a ton of direction from my own marathon training. I started with an LLM writing the plans, but that got expensive and unreliable, so I switched to a rules-based engine. Then I ran real runners' histories through it, had LLMs grade the plans against the research and suggest changes, and reviewed every round myself.

Claude Code wrote all of the code. My part was the product, the research and the model. I built it from January to April while working full time and got a group of runners from my run club using it. I still tinker with it and use it to make training plans.

5K 5 Bars

Race-day app for our run club's bar crawl

Status
Built and live; race date TBD
Built
Sep 2026
Link
Try the demo
Built with
Claude Code, Supabase, MapLibre, GitHub Pages

I'm organizing a 5K 5 Bars for the Run & Tri Club with the Beer Club: about a kilometer of running between each of five bars, from Rittenhouse Square to Morgan's Pier. I wanted a way to know who actually made it to each bar, so I built an app for race day.

You join with your bib number and first name. When you walk into a bar you tap I'm at the bar, and it checks your phone's location. When you head out you tap Leaving. The leaderboard ranks everyone on running pace only, so time at the bars never counts and drinking fast doesn't help you. If someone forgets a tap, that leg just doesn't count. It never guesses a time. GPS is often off inside bars, so if the app says you're too far away you can still check in. That check-in counts and is marked as not GPS-checked so the organizer can see it.

People will be a few drinks in at a loud bar, so the race screen only shows the next bar, one big button and when the group is leaving. The stats wait for the finish screen. It only reads your location when you tap, and it saves how far you were from the bar, never where you were.

Before building I wrote down who uses it and when, what every screen shows in every situation, the look, and a log of every decision. Claude Code reads those before it changes anything. I’d been planning it for a while, so once I sat down to build, the first version came together in a day.

I built In. so Wharton and Penn grad students can find someone to run with. You post a run the night before with the time, the pace and where it starts, and people tap I'm in or Maybe. It shows who's going and what time to leave your place, and every run has its own chat.

The first version of In. matched runners using Nash bargaining. I kept this one simple on purpose, so people start using it without much to think about. I have more ideas, but I want to see what I learn from how people use this before deciding how to build out the matching.

A proposed run only happens if the host confirms it by 10 PM. You pick whether friends, friends of friends or all of Wharton can see it, and nobody sees where you live, just where the run starts. Until 20 real people sign up, famous Philly names like Ben Franklin and Jalen Hurts fill out the runs, tagged as bots.

I’d worked through a few drafts of In. before this one, so once I knew what to build, this version took a day with Claude. It's a live app with sign-in, a database and notifications. I'm sharing it with the Run & Tri Club board first.

Wharton has five course sites, two inboxes, two calendars and a lot of club email. I set up Claude Code to keep track of all of it, and every morning it writes me a brief of what's due and what's coming up.

Walldrobe

Rent the Runway, for art

Status
Class venture
Started
Sep 2026
Stage
Pitch done; first interest test done

I bought a print in 2022 that I love. It has hung in three apartments and never looked right. My wall in New York took two years and a lot of mistakes to get right, and almost every mistake was size and spacing, not taste.

The idea is that you'd scan your apartment, mark the art you already own, and swipe through prints to show what you like. Walldrobe would design each wall and send framed prints with a template for hanging them. After that the frames stay up and you swap the prints whenever you want. You could also rent an original from a local artist, and every payment would count toward buying it.

To test it, I offered prints in my New York building for anyone to borrow, and 15 neighbors messaged me. Next is a paid swap test and signing the first Philadelphia artist.
