6:30 PM, at WhartonClose(Esc)

The photo finder

I named everyone in one group photo a day, and Claude found us in the rest by backpack and outfit, even from behind. Each photo lands on my Strava route by time.

This is a slimmed-down version for this site. The one I shared with the group has everyone’s names and every photo. Here everyone else is an initial, photos where anyone else faces the camera are left out, and the counts are for this slice.

Tap a dot on the route to see the photos taken there.

      The calls I made

      1. Face recognition fails from behind, and a lot of our shots were from behind. So I skipped it. Everyone kept their own backpack and mostly one outfit a day, so I named everyone in one group photo per day, and Claude tagged who’s in every photo by backpack and outfit.

      2. Facing the camera and from behind are different searches. Every person in every photo is tagged face, side, from behind or hidden, and the tags it isn’t sure of get a question mark.

      3. My photos had times, and so did my Strava track. Each photo’s time is matched to my track for that day, so it lands on the route with its altitude. That works for other people’s photos too, since the group moved together.

      4. A friend wants the photos of the two of us together. Tap both of us.

      5. Straight off the camera, a lot of photos look worse than they are. Every photo is scored for what it could be after editing, with a reason and an edit note: the crop, the light and the color.

      6. A burst leaves a pile of near-copies. They stack into one, and the best frame is picked.

      Of all 1,644 photos, 207 have me in them and 330 are just scenery. I shared it with the rest of the group.
